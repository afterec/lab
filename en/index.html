02 · THE LAB
What the Lab is

Learn from industry professionals.

AfteREC Lab is a service that teaches you how to produce content with cinema-grade quality: good framing, good lighting, good sets, good preparation and the right equipment for each project. AfteREC does not shoot or deliver video: it gives the direction, and the learner executes. The format is a conversational lesson, taught by professionals working in film: AfteREC has a network of specialists in every area and, for each need, finds the best teacher. Building and dressing a set? The right art director steps in, in a mini lesson that starts from what the student needs and helps them build it.

And the best teacher is not always the one with the most awarded portfolio: it is the one who communicates and explains best, because that is what guarantees the student leaves knowing how to do it. AfteREC always looks for both: the best work and the best way to teach it. The Lab serves content creators, in-house marketing teams, companies and founders: those who already produce and want to raise the quality, and those just starting out, without spending resources where they are not needed.

Why this matters

Producing is no longer optional. Producing badly is expensive.

Video has become the default format of communication: 91% of companies use video as a marketing tool (Wyzowl), and creators have never published so much. But the race for volume has a hidden cost: 62% of consumers develop a negative perception of the brand when they see a low-quality video (Brightcove), and most stop engaging afterwards.

The problem is rarely the equipment: it is the lack of direction. Badly placed light, echoing sound, the wrong format for the channel, scripts that hold no one. Every piece of content published is either building or eroding the image of whoever publishes it, and the difference between one and the other is knowing what you are doing.

04 · WHY AFTEREC
Why work with us

The people who teach are the people who produce.

The direction the student receives in the Lab comes from people who spend the rest of the week on set: AfteREC produces films, music videos and content for companies, and it is that real experience that goes into every session, not theory lifted from a course. The teachers look at the student's examples, see what is holding the quality back and say exactly what to change.

The Lab's philosophy is teaching you not to overspend. A project does not need to be elaborate or full of complex ideas to have impact: often a simple project has exactly the intended effect. The service helps the student articulate the idea, understand what makes a piece of work more or less expensive, and decide what is worth asking a production company for, and what they can do on their own.

05 · HOW IT WORKS
How it works

Three sessions, one executable plan.

1 · Diagnosis

20 to 30 minutes. Analysis of the student's examples, identification of the priority problem and first recommendations. At the end, the student decides whether to continue.

2 · Development

60 to 90 minutes. Format, script structure, set, light, sound and publishing calendar. The student receives the first materials to start working with.

3 · Wrap-up and roadmap

60 to 90 minutes. The plan is tuned to what the student really wants, with the final materials and success criteria.

2 to 3 week cycleonline sessions by default

The sessions are spaced out so the student can test what they are learning between them. They are online by default, which lowers the cost; in person is possible, with travel added. After the cycle there are one-off extra sessions, for those who want to keep going. And when the diagnosis shows the project really calls for professional production, AfteREC can do it: it is the same standard it applies in film, music videos and content for companies.

06 · WHAT YOU GET
What you get

Simple documents, made to be followed.

At the end of the cycle, the student keeps materials made to be used, not filed away: the written diagnosis on one page; the executable roadmap with format, base script, technical setup, calendar and priorities; the equipment and crew list with reference prices, to know exactly how much each type of project costs to produce; and the set map, when it applies, with the recommended capture setup.

Everything in simple documents, with the instructions made clear: what needs to be done, what you want to do and how to do it. It is a plan any production team can pick up, starting with the student's own.
